About

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Yale University, advised by Prof. Dionysis Kalogerias. My work centers on constraint optimization. Especially the dynamics of, and optimization over, probability measures — drawing on stochastic optimization, statistical learning, duality theory, optimal transport, and information geometry.

Before Yale, I completed dual B.Sc. degrees in Computer Engineering and Pure Mathematics at Sharif University of Technology. Earlier, I ranked 9th nationwide in Iran’s National University Entrance Examination and earned a silver medal in the Iranian National Mathematical Olympiad.

Research

My research is organized around four interconnected directions, connecting stochastic optimization, statistical learning, convex and nonconvex duality, information geometry, and decision-making under uncertainty.

01

Distributionally Robust Optimization

Learning and decision-making when the data-generating distribution is uncertain or misspecified — ambiguity sets, risk measures, optimal transport, and dual reformulations that yield robust, statistically meaningful procedures.

02

Federated Learning

Distributed learning with decentralized data — partial participation, client sampling bias, aggregation under distributional mismatch, and optimal-transport methods that align training dynamics with the intended global objective.

03

Constrained Learning & Duality

Constrained statistical learning beyond convexity — fairness, safety, coverage, and risk constraints — and when Lagrangian methods are justified via zero duality gap, dense hypothesis classes, and universal PAC learnability.

04

Dynamics of Sampling

How sampling mechanisms shape learning and long-run statistical behavior — non-uniform participation, stochastic approximation, sampling-induced distribution shift, and gradient flows over probability measures.
